1. WHAT THE APP DOES
------------------------------------------------------------

Ultra Qwen Image Edit Prompt Selector v4 helps you assemble an image-editing instruction
by clicking prepared presets instead of writing every prompt from scratch.
Choose how many input images you will use, browse an editing category, select
one or more instructions, inspect the finished English prompt, and copy it.

Typical editing goals include:

- Changing a hairstyle, hair color, makeup, or a facial detail.
- Replacing an outfit or changing a garment's fabric or pattern.
- Applying a pose, gesture, gaze direction, or composition from a reference.
- Replacing the setting, changing the time of day, or adjusting lighting.
- Refining hands, cleaning edges, removing distractions, or restoring detail.
- Combining different visual references when two or three images are available.

Key characteristics of v4:

- The person or scene being edited is always image1.
- In multi-image mode, image2 and image3 supply reference information.
- The old manual Base / Reference role dropdown is not used.
- Built-in presets are separated into 1-, 2-, and 3-image collections.
- Different editing actions use suitable verbs, not one generic command.
- Compatible instructions can coexist; some conflicting instructions are
  resolved by the prompt builder before being included in the final output.
- Adult-oriented presets are filtered through the NSFW switch and retain their
  ordinary category identifiers internally.
- You can add temporary instructions and save reusable custom presets.
- The bilingual switch changes what the app displays, not its editing logic.

Use the preview as your final source of truth. The app supplies structured text,
not a guarantee that a particular model run will obey every requested change.

4. IMAGE ROLES: IMAGE1, IMAGE2, IMAGE3
------------------------------------------------------------

The image-numbering convention is the most important rule in this application.

ONE IMAGE:
  image1 = the original person, object, or scene to edit.
  No additional reference image is required.

TWO IMAGES:
  image1 = the original image to edit.
  image2 = the reference image providing the requested feature.

THREE IMAGES:
  image1 = the original image to edit.
  image2 = the first reference image.
  image3 = the second reference image.

The *meaning* of image2 and image3 depends on the preset. In one preset, image2
may supply clothing; in another, the hairstyle, background, or lighting.
Their roles are not permanently assigned to fixed categories.

Example:
  Apply the outfit from image2 and the pose from image3 to image1.
  Keep the identity in image1 unchanged.

Here the final output is based on image1. Image2 supplies the outfit, and
image3 supplies the pose. The instruction does not tell Qwen to modify image2
or image3 as separate final images.

CRITICAL: Load the images into Qwen in precisely the same order. If you swap
image2 and image3 in your workflow, the model may use the wrong reference.

7. USING ONE-IMAGE MODE
------------------------------------------------------------

Choose 1 image when all changes are made inside the original image and no
separate reference is needed.

Examples:
  - Change hair color to blonde or brown.
  - Give the person a shorter hairstyle.
  - Change clothing to a chosen outfit style.
  - Replace the background with a beach or studio.
  - Make the lighting warmer or cooler.
  - Refine skin, hands, or garment edges.
  - Remove an unwanted object or watermark.
  - Repair a small damaged or occluded region.

Illustrative prompt structure:
  Change only the hair color in image1 to blonde.
  Keep the face, hairstyle, pose, clothing, background, lighting, and framing
  in image1 unchanged.

The intent is to change a narrowly specified feature while retaining the rest.
Some single-image presets naturally say "the person" or "the subject" instead
of repeatedly saying image1. That is part of the original prompt structure,
not an error in the image numbering.

A useful first test is a simple hair-color change. It reveals whether your
model is following both the requested edit and the preservation conditions.


8. USING TWO-IMAGE MODE
------------------------------------------------------------

Choose 2 images when image1 is the original and image2 provides a reference.

Common goals:
  - Apply the face or facial identity from image2 to image1.
  - Dress the person in image1 in the outfit from image2.
  - Transfer only hairstyle, hair color, or makeup from image2.
  - Match a pose, body angle, or gaze direction shown in image2.
  - Use image2 as a reference for background, lighting, or color mood.

Illustrative prompt structure:
  Replace only the face in image1 with the face from image2.
  Keep the hairstyle, hair color, pose, clothing, background, lighting, and
  framing in image1 unchanged.

For face references, choose a photo with a clearly visible face. For clothing,
choose one that shows the required garment and its details. For a pose, prefer
a reference where the limbs and body direction are readable.

Ask a simple question before copying: "Am I transferring the entire feature,
or only one detail?" For example, a hair-color transfer should not
accidentally become a complete hairstyle transfer.


9. USING THREE-IMAGE MODE
------------------------------------------------------------

Choose 3 images when the edit draws separately from two references.

Examples:
  - Outfit from image2 + pose from image3.
  - Makeup from image2 + hair color from image3.
  - Background architecture from image2 + lighting from image3.
  - Object from image2 + hand position from image3.
  - Visual style from image2 + framing from image3.

Illustrative prompt structure:
  Apply the outfit from image2 and the pose from image3 to image1.
  Preserve the identity of the person in image1.

Read each multi-image preset carefully. Image2 is not always "the outfit
reference," and image3 is not always "the pose reference." Those are just
useful examples. The particular preset text defines the actual roles.

Three-image edits require more coordination than one-image edits. If a result
mixes the references incorrectly, try one transfer at a time or use more
specific presets with explicit "from image2" / "from image3" wording.


10. HOW TO READ THE GENERATED PROMPT
------------------------------------------------------------

The preview is the English text you should provide to Qwen Image Edit.
Understanding a few recurring phrases makes it easier to review.

  Apply A from image2 to image1
    Transfer feature A from the reference into the original.

  Apply A from image2 and B from image3 to image1
    Use a different source image for each of two features.

  Keep ... unchanged / Preserve ...
    Ask the model to retain specific features from the original.

  Only / Change only
    Limit the edit to a narrower part of the image.

  Match
    Adjust an attribute in image1 to resemble the reference attribute.

  Remove / Clean up / Repair / Refine
    Remove something, reduce visual artifacts, or restore details.

  Replace / Dress / Rebuild
    Make a more substantial change than a small adjustment.

Before exporting, check four things:
  1) Which part of image1 is changing?
  2) Which features come from image2 or image3?
  3) Which aspects of image1 should stay the same?
  4) Are any instructions mutually exclusive?

Longer prompts are not automatically better. A short, coherent instruction
can outperform several competing edits.


11. SELECTED INSTRUCTIONS AND CONFLICT HANDLING
------------------------------------------------------------

You can select multiple presets. The builder applies internal rules to
reduce certain conflicts before producing the final prompt.

Examples likely to conflict:
  - Change hair color to black AND change it to blonde.
  - Replace the background with a beach AND replace it with an indoor room.
  - Replace the entire outfit AND preserve the entire old outfit.
  - Use two different full-body poses as the main pose.

An example status might look like:
  Selected 3 · Applied 2 · Resolved 1 conflict(s)

This means you clicked three items but only two effective instructions are
being applied after conflict handling. The selected chips may still show an
excluded choice so you can review and remove it yourself.

Examples that can often coexist:
  - Background location + weather effect.
  - Overall outfit + a small clothing detail.
  - Body orientation + direction of gaze.
  - Pose change + facial expression adjustment.

IMPORTANT: This is rule-based conflict resolution, not a model interpreting
your personal intent. Read the final preview even if the status says there
are no conflicts. Selection order can affect which incompatible instruction
wins. Reduce the number of changes when a result seems inconsistent.

Useful English search terms:
  face, hair, blonde, outfit, dress, pose, background, lighting, makeup,

them, including Korean descriptions stored behind the English interface.

14. USING THE NSFW FILTER
------------------------------------------------------------

Click the NSFW control in the category row to browse adult-oriented presets.
Click it again to return to the general SFW collection.

How the filter works:
  - SFW shows the general presets, while NSFW shows the adult set.
  - Adult presets still belong to ordinary internal categories such as
    Clothing, Pose, Background, Details, or Interaction.
  - The internal marker for those presets is nsfw:true, not cat:'nsfw'.
  - Changing SFW/NSFW does NOT automatically delete already selected presets.
  - Consequently, a selection made in one filter can remain in the final
    prompt even when its card is no longer visible in the current filter.

To avoid surprises, inspect Selected Instructions and the preview whenever
you switch modes. If a hidden-by-filter selection is no longer wanted,
remove it using the X on its selected instruction.

Adult-oriented presets are intended only for clearly adult subjects.
The capabilities and restrictions of individual models and platforms vary.
The app creates a text instruction; it cannot guarantee that a requested
edit will be accepted or rendered as expected by a downstream service.


15. QUICK ENTRY: ONE-TIME INSTRUCTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

Quick Entry is the small direct-input section beneath the preset selection.
It is for temporary additions to the current working prompt.

Example English input:
  Add small gold earrings to the person in image1.

After clicking + Add, the instruction becomes part of the selected prompt.
It will appear in the preview and be included in Copy and TXT export.

Quick Entry DOES NOT:
  - Save a reusable preset to the browser's custom list.
  - Insert anything into an HTML file via Permanent Save.
  - Replace the Custom Preset Manager.

In KO mode, a separate Korean note can help you recognize the temporary
selection on screen. In EN mode that Korean field is hidden and not used.

Quick Entry also checks for equivalent selected English instructions before
adding an exact or normalized duplicate. If it reports that the instruction
is already selected, remove the original or change your input.

For anything you will use repeatedly, create a saved custom preset instead.


16. TWO TYPES OF SAVING: BROWSER AND HTML
------------------------------------------------------------

A) BROWSER STORAGE (localStorage)

Custom presets added in Custom Preset Manager are saved to the current
browser's local storage under the qwenCustoms key.

Advantages:
  - Fast and convenient for testing and ongoing use.
  - Does not modify the HTML source file on disk.

Limitations:
  - Browser data cleanup may delete stored presets.
  - Private/incognito sessions may not retain the data.
  - Another browser, computer, or file security context may not share it.

B) PERMANENT SAVE INTO HTML

The Permanent Save action reads an HTML file you select and inserts your saved
custom presets into its EDITS_1, EDITS_2, and EDITS_3 source arrays.

Advantages:
  - The resulting HTML file carries the embedded custom presets with it.
  - You can back it up or copy it to another computer.

Limitations:
  - It modifies or creates an HTML file, depending on browser capabilities.
  - It requires a backup in case you choose the wrong file or later want to
    roll back your changes.
  - Temporary Quick Entry text is never included.

Recommended approach: test presets in browser storage, then use Permanent
Save only after the results and wording have been verified.

18. WRITING USEFUL CUSTOM ENGLISH INSTRUCTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

A good custom preset answers three questions:
  What should change? Where does the reference come from? What must remain?

Prefer:
  Apply the hairstyle from image2 to image1.
  Preserve the face identity, hair color, pose, clothing, and background
  of the person in image1.

Avoid:
  Make it like the reference.

The vague version does not identify the source or the target.

For three-image combinations, identify each source separately:
  Apply the makeup style from image2 and the hair color from image3 to image1.
  Preserve the facial identity in image1.

Useful command verbs:
  Change / Replace — make a requested change or substitution.
  Apply / Transfer — bring an attribute from a specific reference image.
  Match / Align — adjust a feature to resemble an example.
  Refine / Repair / Clean up — correct quality, structure, or finish.
  Remove — take an unwanted object or artifact away.
  Add / Place — insert a requested object, detail, or element.

Use "only" for narrow edits, but do not overuse constraints that contradict
the requested change. A preset that says "replace the outfit" and "keep
the outfit unchanged" is internally inconsistent.

The app's English UI does not translate your custom English text. Write it
in the form you want the prompt generator to use.


19. PERMANENTLY EMBEDDING CUSTOM PRESETS IN HTML
------------------------------------------------------------

Permanent Save makes an HTML copy containing the custom presets you registered
in Custom Preset Manager. Quick Entry is excluded.

Safe procedure:
  1) Keep the ZIP and unmodified bilingual HTML as backups.
  2) Add your custom presets and test their category and image count.
  3) Check the final prompt using each new preset.
  4) Click Permanently Save to HTML in the manager.
  5) Select the BILINGUAL v4 HTML from this package, not an older Korean-only
     HTML. The bilingual app rejects source files missing its language switch.
  6) Follow the browser prompt to overwrite, save a copy, or download a file.
  7) Locate the actual newly saved file and open it in the browser.
  8) Confirm that the custom presets are now embedded and the KO/EN switch
     still works in the saved version.

After successful saving, the manager clears its browser-managed custom list
to avoid duplicate entries when the saved HTML is reopened. Selected custom
chips may also be removed for consistency, so reopen the final file to test.

If you cancel the file picker, no permanent save is performed. If the browser
cannot overwrite the original, use the downloaded or Save As version instead.

Do not assume that the original HTML on disk was overwritten just because a
save message appeared. Check its location and reopen the file you intend to
use. An older HTML copy can silently confuse which edition is running.


20. RESETTING SELECTIONS AND CUSTOM PRESETS
------------------------------------------------------------

The app has different reset operations. They do not do the same thing.

RESET NEAR THE PROMPT PREVIEW
  Clears the current editing selection and returns the working prompt to an
  unselected state. It does not erase saved custom presets from the manager.

RESET IN CUSTOM PRESET MANAGER
  After confirmation, clears user-managed custom presets from browser storage.
  It does not remove built-in presets embedded in the HTML file.

What if a preset is already embedded in an HTML file?
  The manager's browser reset cannot undo the HTML source modification.
  Reopen an older backup HTML, or carefully remove that entry from the EDITS
  array in a text editor if you understand JavaScript syntax.

Avoid using reset as a substitute for removing just one instruction. For a
single unwanted selection, use the selected-chip X button instead.

21. COPY, TXT EXPORT, AND KOREAN TRANSLATION
------------------------------------------------------------

COPY
  Copies the currently composed English prompt to your clipboard. Paste it
  into your Qwen Image Edit workflow's text prompt input. If clipboard access
  is restricted, follow the app's copy-failure notice and copy manually.

TXT
  Downloads the currently composed English prompt as a text file, typically
  named qwen_prompt.txt. This is useful for comparing runs, preserving a good
  instruction, or recording the prompt used for a particular result.

TRANSLATE (KO mode)
  Opens a Korean translation view for the finished English prompt. This is a
  convenience for checking meaning, not a change to the underlying text.
  The translation feature may need Internet access or a third-party service.

In EN mode the translation control is hidden. The prompt is already English,
so you can immediately copy it or export it as TXT.

A successful Copy or TXT action does not save custom preset definitions. To
keep your reusable instructions, use Custom Preset Manager and Permanent Save.


22. USING THE PROMPT IN A QWEN IMAGE EDIT WORKFLOW
------------------------------------------------------------

A typical workflow is:

1) Open your Qwen Image Edit interface or compatible ComfyUI workflow.
2) Supply one, two, or three images in the required input slots.
3) In this app, select the matching image-count mode.
4) Choose the presets that describe your desired change.
5) Read the final English preview and any warnings.
6) Click Copy and paste the prompt into the model's text input.
7) Run the image-edit model using your usual settings.
8) Compare the output with the intended edit and its preservation conditions.

The number printed in an instruction is meaningful, not decorative.

If a three-image preset uses:
  outfit from image2, pose from image3

then configure the model input images as:
  First:  original photo to edit (image1)
  Second: outfit reference (image2)
  Third:  pose reference (image3)

The app does not inspect your ComfyUI graph or rearrange model input slots.
You are responsible for making the order in your workflow match the prompt.

A structurally correct prompt cannot compensate for a workflow that supplies
images in the wrong order or uses a model without the needed capabilities.


23. FIVE PRACTICAL EDITING RECIPES
------------------------------------------------------------

A. TRANSFER A FACE

  Image count: 2
  image1: the original image to change
  image2: a clear face reference
  Category: Face & Hair
  Choose a preset that transfers the facial identity from image2 to image1.
  Check that the generated text preserves hairstyle, pose, clothing, and
  background if you do not want them changed.

B. TRANSFER AN OUTFIT

  Image count: 2
  image1: person wearing the original outfit
  image2: photo showing the desired outfit
  Category: Clothing
  Choose a preset for outfit transfer. Read whether it requests the whole
  garment or just a material, pattern, color, or accessory.

C. COMBINE OUTFIT AND POSE

  Image count: 3
  image1: original person to edit
  image2: outfit reference
  image3: pose reference
  Category: Clothing or Pose
  Choose a preset that explicitly uses image2 for clothing and image3 for
  pose. Verify that the identity in image1 is preserved if needed.

D. CHANGE ONLY THE BACKGROUND

  Image count: 1 for a preset-based new location, or 2 if a specific
               background reference is available.
  Category: Background
  Check that the subject, face, outfit, and pose are retained. If only the
  lighting should change, use Lighting instead of replacing the whole scene.

E. REMOVE A DISTRACTING OBJECT

  Image count: 1 for a simple cleanup; 2 or 3 only when the selected preset
               explicitly needs additional texture or lighting references.
  Category: Details
  Keep the request focused on the unwanted object and check that the model
  is not also being asked to redesign the surroundings.

These are examples of how to choose presets, not promises that a single run
will always reproduce the intended result perfectly.


24. TIPS FOR MORE RELIABLE PROMPTS
------------------------------------------------------------

- Start with the most important edit, not a long list of minor improvements.
- Test a single face, clothing, pose, or background change before mixing them.
- Add style, lighting, or cleanup refinements only after the core edit works.
- Keep the reference images focused on the features you want to transfer.
- Verify every "from image2" and "from image3" source in the preview.
- Look for Keep / Preserve clauses covering the parts you want unchanged.
- Avoid requesting two different whole-scene changes at the same time.
- For complex editing, save a good intermediate output and use it as the next
  image1 in a second pass, rather than making every change in one step.
- When comparing two prompt strategies, keep the model/workflow settings as
  consistent as possible so that differences are easier to interpret.

More text does not always mean more control. The goal is one coherent editing
instruction with clear sources, targets, and preservation conditions.


25. UNDERSTANDING PRESET LABELS AND DESCRIPTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

In KO mode, a card displays its English instruction and a Korean explanation.
The Korean line is designed to describe the intended edit clearly; it is not
necessarily a word-for-word translation of the English sentence.

In EN mode, the same card displays only the English instruction. This avoids
showing a Korean description under thousands of English-language presets.

To understand a long or technical English preset:

1) Read its action verb: Change, Apply, Replace, Match, Refine, or Remove.
2) Identify which image supplies the feature and which image receives it.
3) Check for "only," which narrows the edit to a specific attribute.
4) Read any Keep / Preserve clause to see what should remain unchanged.
5) Select the item, then read its contribution in the final preview.
6) Deselect it if the resulting instruction does not match your intent.

Example:
  "Apply only the hair color from image2 to image1"
  changes a hair-color attribute, rather than necessarily copying the entire
  hairstyle from the reference.

Long English chips in the selected list may be visually truncated to maintain
a neat layout. Inspect the full text via the tooltip or preview instead of
judging the instruction only by the clipped label.

32. HOW VERSION 4 COMPOSES A PROMPT
------------------------------------------------------------

Each preset contains an English instruction already written for an editing
task. The app does more than concatenate the translated names of selected
categories. Its prompt builder processes compatible and incompatible edits.

The general flow is:

1) Determine the image-count mode and the selected presets.
2) Identify which image is edited and which references provide attributes.
3) Resolve certain conflicts between instructions for the same attribute.
4) Keep compatible, narrower edits where possible.
5) Avoid preservation clauses that directly contradict requested changes.
6) Where appropriate, add or retain preservation guidance.
7) Compose the resulting English editing instructions.
8) Use the same result for preview, clipboard Copy, and TXT export.

Compatible example:
  Replace the background with a winter forest.
  Add gentle snowfall.

These can often coexist because one defines a location and the other adds
a weather effect.

Likely conflict:
  Change the hair color to black.
  Change the hair color to blonde.

Both target the same attribute with different values. A later selection may
take priority; check which instruction appears in the final prompt.

Changing KO to EN never switches to a different prompt-building engine.


33. READING A PRESET AS A COMPLETE EDITING PLAN
------------------------------------------------------------

A good preset communicates at least some of the following:

  TARGET: What is being edited in image1?
  SOURCE: Which reference (image2 or image3) supplies the feature?
  SCOPE: Is this a complete replacement or a narrowly targeted adjustment?
  PRESERVATION: What must remain consistent with image1?

Example 1:
  Apply only the hair color from image2 to image1.

Interpretation:
  - image1 remains the edited image.
  - image2 supplies color, not necessarily haircut or identity.
  - The word "only" limits the intended transfer.

Example 2:
  Apply the face from image2 and the hairstyle from image3 to image1.
  Preserve the pose, clothing, and background in image1.

Interpretation:
  - Face and hairstyle come from different references.
  - The final subject remains in the scene from image1.
  - Pose, clothing, and background are supposed to stay as before.

Do not assume that a short visible chip contains the complete instruction.
Read the preview and, when available, the full card tooltip.


34. PREPARING THE ORIGINAL IMAGE (IMAGE1)
------------------------------------------------------------

Image1 determines the foundation of the output. A good original makes specific
edits easier for the model to perform.

For face editing:
  Use an image where the original face is reasonably clear and large enough.

For clothing editing:
  Keep the body and clothing area visible. Cropped garments may be harder to
  replace accurately or match to a detailed reference.

For pose editing:
  A visible body outline and unoccluded limbs help the model interpret the
  person whose pose you want to change.

For background editing:
  A clear subject/background boundary can make it easier to change the
  surroundings while retaining the subject.

For hands or small objects:
  Higher resolution and clear subject boundaries generally help correction.

If a crucial part of image1 is heavily obscured or missing, a good reference
alone may not recover it. Consider repairing the affected area in a separate
pass before applying another major visual change.


35. PREPARING REFERENCE IMAGES (IMAGE2 AND IMAGE3)
------------------------------------------------------------

References work best when the feature you want is obvious.

FACE REFERENCE
  - Choose a sufficiently clear and unobstructed face.
  - Very small faces give the model less usable detail.
  - A very different camera angle can complicate identity transfer.

CLOTHING REFERENCE
  - Show enough of the full outfit to establish design and fit.
  - For fabric or pattern transfer, make sure that detail is visible.
  - A complicated reference pose may influence clothing transfer indirectly.

POSE REFERENCE
  - Show the body direction, limb positions, and intended gesture clearly.
  - Extreme differences from image1's camera perspective can cause an
    unintended composition change.
  - Small hand gestures benefit from detail and resolution.

BACKGROUND REFERENCE
  - Decide whether you want architecture, location, colors, or atmosphere.
  - A clean reference may help when replacing the whole background.
  - Choose a background-specific preset if you do not also want lighting or
    style from the reference to be copied.

LIGHTING REFERENCE
  - Prefer an image with a clear light direction, strength, or color.
  - For simple brightness adjustments, avoid presets that also transfer the
    entire look or scene unless that is the desired effect.

In 3-image mode, write down the intended role of image2 and image3 before
choosing presets. It is easy to reverse sources when both references are busy.


36. COMBINING MULTIPLE PRESETS INTENTIONALLY
------------------------------------------------------------

Several compatible presets can provide more control than one broad command,
but adding everything at once is usually counterproductive.

Often useful combinations:
  - Full outfit change + a specific garment pattern.
  - New background location + snowfall or rain.
  - Body pose + direction of gaze.
  - Face transfer + hairstyle transfer from a different reference.
  - Style treatment + fine detail cleanup.
  - Lighting change + shadow refinement.

Combinations that require special care:
  - Face replacement + preserve the entire original face identity.
  - Full outfit replacement + keep the original complete outfit.
  - Whole-body pose transfer + preserve the full original pose.
  - Two competing background locations.
  - Two different final hair colors.
  - Conflicting camera views or framing requests.

Suggested starting points:
  - Simple edit: one or two presets.
  - Two references: one combined preset, or a small group of related edits.
  - Complex work: test a few instructions first, then refine in later passes.

The app can resolve some conflicts automatically, but it cannot judge every
semantic overlap or quality tradeoff. The final prompt still needs your review.


37. INTERPRETING THE STATUS AND WARNING AREA
------------------------------------------------------------

The preview status provides counts of the selected and effective instructions.

Example:
  Selected 3 · Applied 3 · No conflicts

All three selected instructions currently contribute to the output.

Example:
  Selected 3 · Applied 2 · Resolved 1 conflict(s)

Three items were selected, but one is excluded by the builder's conflict
handling. The warning can help identify recently excluded instructions.

Other reasons for an advisory warning include:
  - References that need checking against the current image-role arrangement.
  - A large number of effective instructions being applied at once.
  - Simultaneous edits across many major visual domains.

These warnings are aids, not absolute predictions of model quality. A clean
status does not prove the prompt is visually sensible. Conversely, a warning
does not necessarily mean the whole workflow will fail.

Always compare the preview with the real image layout before copying.

39. ADULT-PRESET WORKFLOWS WITH ONE IMAGE
------------------------------------------------------------

One-image NSFW mode edits an adult subject or adult-oriented scene already
present in image1. The same image1 target convention still applies.

Common categories of changes include:
  - Clothing presentation and coverage.
  - Adult pose or general body presentation.
  - Bedroom, bath, sauna, or studio settings.
  - Warm, moody, or dramatic lighting.
  - Skin texture, anatomy cleanup, or other details.

Practical guidance:
  - Keep clothing, pose, and scene edits manageable rather than stacking many
    large changes in a single pass.
  - For fine detail, use a source image where the relevant area is visible.
  - A scene-wide change and a small detail correction may be more reliable as
    two separate passes.

40. ADULT-PRESET WORKFLOWS WITH TWO IMAGES
------------------------------------------------------------

In two-image NSFW mode:
  image1 = adult subject or scene being edited.
  image2 = reference for clothing, pose, appearance, interaction layout,
           background, lighting, or another explicitly named attribute.

An interaction preset may treat two adults already present in image1 as the
editing targets and use image2 as a reference for their relative position.
Another preset may use image2 only for a garment's design or material.

Before using the preset, answer:
  - Are you transferring the reference person's identity or only a pose?
  - Is the reference used for clothing, scene layout, or lighting?
  - Does the prompt specify which people in image1 are being changed?

Read the actual English preview to distinguish these cases. Do not infer
that image2 always means "second person"; it is simply the reference image.


41. ADULT-PRESET WORKFLOWS WITH THREE IMAGES
------------------------------------------------------------

Three-image NSFW mode supports editing adult subjects with two distinct
references. The same input-role principle applies as in general mode.

  image1 = final editing target.
  image2 = first adult subject or visual-reference source.
  image3 = second subject, pose, composition, or other reference source.

Possible preset structures include:

A) Transfer styling from image2 and pose information from image3 to image1.

B) Use a person from image2 in the composition of image1, with image3
   supplying an arrangement or pose reference.

C) Use people or visual attributes from image2 and image3 in a final scene
   composed around image1, when the chosen preset explicitly requests it.

The critical rule is that image2 and image3 supply information to the final
result in image1. They are not necessarily being edited as separate outputs.

Group compositions, occlusion, and multiple faces increase difficulty. Confirm
that the prompt names the intended people and preserves their identities when
needed. Separate a complex edit into stages if a single pass misplaces people
or mixes source features.

43. VERIFY IMAGE NUMBERS IN YOUR CUSTOM TEXT
------------------------------------------------------------

The app supports one-, two-, and three-image modes, and image1 is the final
editing target. Your custom prompt should follow these conventions:

ONE-IMAGE CUSTOM PRESET
  Use image1 (or clearly refer to the original subject). Do not describe
  image2 or image3 unless those images will actually be present.

TWO-IMAGE CUSTOM PRESET
  Use image1 for the editing target and image2 for the reference. Do not
  mention image3 if your workflow will supply only two input images.

THREE-IMAGE CUSTOM PRESET
  image1 = target, image2 and image3 = available references. image4 is not
  part of this application's three-image workflow.

Good example:
  Apply the face from image2 to the person in image1.

Incomplete example:
  Apply the face from image2.

The second example leaves the target unspecified.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION OF THIS RELEASE:
  The current Custom Preset Manager validates the image count field, category,
  and required English text. It does NOT directly reject every out-of-range
  image-number token written *inside* a custom English instruction.
  In particular, do not rely on the Add Preset button to detect image4 or
  a missing image1 reference. Check your text manually before saving and
  before running a model. This manual describes the behavior of this ZIP,
  rather than claiming a validation guard that is not implemented here.

The main prompt generator may normalize some instruction wording, but it is
not a substitute for checking custom reference-image numbers yourself.


44. FIVE RULES FOR CLEAR CUSTOM PROMPTS
------------------------------------------------------------

RULE 1 — NAME THE TARGET DIRECTLY

Better:
  Change the hair color in image1 to warm brown.

Vague:
  Change the subject.

RULE 2 — PUT THE REFERENCE SOURCE NEAR THE FEATURE

Better:
  Apply the outfit from image2 to the person in image1.

Vague:
  Apply the outfit while preserving image1 from image2.

RULE 3 — SEPARATE TWO REFERENCE ROLES

Better:
  Apply the outfit from image2 to image1.
  Set the pose in image1 to match the pose from image3.

Vague:
  Combine image2 and image3.

RULE 4 — DISTINGUISH COMPLETE REPLACEMENT FROM PARTIAL CHANGE

  Replace / Change the entire ... = broad substitution.
  Apply only / Match only ...    = limited feature transfer.
  Refine / Repair / Clean up     = correction or improvement.
  Remove                        = subtraction.
  Add / Place                   = insertion.

RULE 5 — DO NOT PRESERVE WHAT YOU ARE ASKING TO REPLACE

Contradictory:
  Replace the clothing in image1. Keep the clothing unchanged.

Clearer:
  Replace the clothing in image1. Keep the face, pose, background, and
  lighting unchanged.

Write natural English commands. You do not need unusually formal wording or
long lists of generic quality adjectives to make a useful editing preset.


45. WRITING A KOREAN DESCRIPTION FOR A CUSTOM PRESET
------------------------------------------------------------

This section matters when your preset will also be used in the KO interface.

The Korean Description field is a display explanation, not the English prompt
that Qwen receives. A useful explanation should convey the actual operation,
including which image supplies the feature and what is preserved.

For example, a good description for a two-image clothing-pattern transfer
would specify that only the pattern from image2 is applied to the clothing
in image1. A description such as "Apply pattern" is much less informative.

A useful three-image explanation identifies both references:
  image2 = facial source, image3 = hairstyle source, image1 = edited target.

Try to include these concepts where relevant:
  - Which part of image1 changes.
  - Which image provides the reference.
  - Whether this is a full replacement or a small adjustment.
  - Which important features are supposed to stay unchanged.

In EN mode, the Korean Description field is intentionally hidden. New
English-only presets use the English instruction as the Korean fallback,
and KO mode suppresses the duplicate display line when both are identical.

When you edit an existing bilingual custom preset in EN mode, a distinct
Korean description is preserved unchanged. It is not automatically translated
to reflect your new English wording. Switch to KO mode if both descriptions
need to be updated together.

47. KOREAN TRANSLATION IS AN AID, NOT THE SOURCE PROMPT
------------------------------------------------------------

In KO mode, the Translate control can help explain the English preview in
Korean. The translated result does not replace the English source instruction.

Automated translation may handle specialized editing terms imperfectly:
  identity    = the subject's recognizable visual identity.
  framing     = how the subject and scene fit inside the image.
  preserve    = maintain a specified aspect of the original.
  transfer    = bring an attribute from a reference into the target.
  match       = make one property resemble a reference property.
  subject     = the person or object being discussed.

If a translation feels unnatural, compare the English source directly with
the selected presets. The wording of a translated explanation alone does not
prove the source editing command is incorrect.

EN mode hides Translate because the preview already appears in English.
This has no effect on Copy, TXT, or the instruction sent to your model.


48. WHEN QWEN DOES NOT FOLLOW THE REQUEST
------------------------------------------------------------

Work through the following checks in order:

1) Verify that the correct number of images reached the workflow.
2) Check the actual order: image1 target, then the intended references.
3) Read the final prompt for reversed image2/image3 sources.
4) Reduce the number of simultaneously selected presets.
5) Try the single most important edit on its own.
6) Review the status area for resolved conflicts or reference warnings.
7) Check that the desired feature is clearly visible in the reference.
8) Compare camera angle and body orientation between target and reference.
9) If face and clothing transfers compete, test them in separate passes.
10) Use a specific partial-change preset instead of a broad replacement.
11) Try another seed if the model's output is inconsistent.

IF THE MODEL CHANGES TOO LITTLE:
  - Consider a more direct replacement instruction.
  - Check whether the chosen preset requests only a small adjustment.
  - Remove contradictory custom Keep / Preserve clauses.

IF THE MODEL CHANGES TOO MUCH:
  - Choose a preset that uses "only" or an explicit limited transfer.
  - Preserve identity, pose, framing, background, or lighting as appropriate.
  - Separate a large change from the smaller corrections that follow it.

IF THE MODEL MIXES THE TWO REFERENCES:
  - Use a preset that explicitly mentions "from image2" and "from image3".
  - Make the intended role of each reference visually obvious.
  - Use staged edits if one pass cannot maintain the distinctions.

A correct prompt can still produce imperfect results because of model,
workflow, image quality, resolution, seed, or available model capabilities.


49. PRACTICAL COMBINATIONS BY CATEGORY
------------------------------------------------------------

FACE & HAIR
  - Transfer the face while keeping hairstyle unchanged.
  - Apply a hairstyle while keeping original hair color.
  - Change eye color while preserving the eye shape.
  - Apply makeup technique without copying unrelated reference details.

CLOTHING
  - Replace the outfit but preserve footwear.
  - Replace only the top while retaining the bottom.
  - Match a silhouette while keeping the original material.
  - Change a pattern while preserving the garment's dominant color.

POSE
  - Transfer an overall pose while retaining face and clothing.
  - Match an arm position without replacing the entire posture.
  - Adjust gaze direction independently from body orientation.
  - Change a hand position to fit an existing prop.

BACKGROUND
  - Replace the location and then add a weather effect.
  - Keep the location but change the time of day.
  - Clean up the scene while retaining its lighting atmosphere.
  - Apply background architecture from image2 and lighting from image3.

LIGHTING
  - Match light direction while preserving the existing background.
  - Adjust color temperature while refining facial exposure.
  - Add rim light without completely changing the light setup.
  - Use image2 for lighting direction and image3 for color mood.

STYLE
  - Change overall rendering style while preserving visual identity.
  - Apply a film look without modifying framing.
  - Apply a painterly treatment while retaining facial structure.
  - Match color grading while keeping shapes and textures readable.

DETAILS
  - Repair hand anatomy while maintaining the intended gesture.
  - Refine garment boundaries without redesigning the clothing.
  - Remove an unwanted object and restore the nearby texture.
  - Use image2 for a clean texture patch and image3 for lighting guidance.

Choose presets according to the current image count; the combinations above
illustrate compatible goals, not necessarily exact built-in preset names.


50. USING A PHONE OR A SMALL BROWSER WINDOW
------------------------------------------------------------

The application is responsive, but a large preset library and long editing
instructions are generally easier to browse on a desktop monitor.

Suggestions for smaller screens:
  - Use Search instead of scrolling through hundreds of presets.
  - Narrow the category before looking for a detailed action.
  - Read a selected instruction's full text if its chip is truncated.
  - Keep the preview visible when finalizing the prompt.
  - Use Copy to move the prompt to your model interface.
  - Expect some categories and manager-table columns to scroll or wrap
    inside their own area rather than forcing the whole page wider.

On mobile, the browser may prompt for clipboard or download permissions.
If an action is restricted, use the available fallback, or perform the
permanent-file operation on a desktop browser where file access is easier.

The KO/EN switch is part of the interface and can be used on small screens.
Switching language should not change the selected English prompt text.


51. BROWSER STORAGE, PRIVACY, AND LOCAL FILES
------------------------------------------------------------

Most prompt-building work happens locally in the browser. You do not upload
an image to this HTML app merely to generate a text prompt.

Browser-managed custom presets can be stored in localStorage. That is
associated with the current browser context and can be lost if site data
is cleared or a private session ends.

Be mindful of these cases:
  - Public/shared computers may retain custom text in browser storage.
  - A locally opened HTML file may have different storage behavior depending
    on browser policy or where the file is opened.
  - Changing browser profile or computer does not necessarily copy presets.
  - A permanently saved HTML contains embedded custom text in readable source.
  - Sharing a saved HTML can share those embedded instructions as well.
  - Optional Korean prompt translation and online font downloads may make
    network requests. Basic prompt generation does not depend on them.

The KO/EN preference is also stored in the browser where storage is available.
It is a display preference, not a language setting for your Qwen model.

53. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

Q. Is image2 always a face reference and image3 always a pose reference?
A. No. The selected preset defines what each reference supplies. Review
   "from image2" and "from image3" in the English prompt.

Q. Do I need ComfyUI installed to use this app?
A. No. The HTML app builds text prompts in a browser. You need a separate
   compatible model or workflow to generate edited images.

Q. Do I get a better result if I select more presets?
A. Not necessarily. Too many competing edits can weaken the result. Begin
   with a small number of clear instructions.

Q. Does switching KO/EN translate or rewrite the Qwen prompt?
A. No. It changes labels, dialogs, and preset-description visibility.
   The generated English instructions remain the same.

Q. Why is there no Korean line below each card in EN mode?
A. EN mode intentionally hides the Korean explanation. The English command
   is both the visible preset text and the source instruction.

Q. Can I search using Korean while EN is selected?
A. Yes. The search continues to match both English and Korean preset data.

Q. Why does the long selected instruction end with an ellipsis?
A. This is a display limit used to keep the layout readable. The complete
   instruction is still available through its tooltip and final preview.

Q. Does turning NSFW off delete any previously selected NSFW preset?
A. No. Switching filters does not automatically remove selections. Review
   the selected chips and preview to see what is still active.

Q. What happens if I click the same preset twice?
A. The first click selects it. Clicking that same selected card again
   generally removes it from the working selection.

Q. What happens when I pick a search result for a different image count?
A. The app switches image-count mode. If existing selections would be
   cleared, it can ask for confirmation. Check the new input-image count.

Q. Can I write "image4" in a custom preset?
A. You should not. This app has at most three input-image modes. The current
   custom manager does not fully validate every numbered token inside the
   English text, so check custom references yourself before saving.

Q. Are custom presets available on another browser or computer?
A. Browser-only entries may not transfer automatically. Embed them into
   a bilingual HTML file using Permanent Save and copy that file.

Q. Why was the Korean description not updated after I edited the English
   instruction in EN mode?
A. This is deliberate. Existing distinct Korean explanations are preserved
   rather than automatically translated. Switch to KO mode to update them.

Q. Why does Permanent Save ask me to choose an HTML file?
A. The app reads the source of the file you choose and embeds your stored
   custom presets in that file's preset arrays. Always choose the bilingual
   version from this package and keep a backup.

Q. Can I undo a permanent HTML save with the Reset button?
A. No. Resetting browser presets does not revert files already modified.
   Restore a backup or edit the source carefully.

Q. My Qwen result ignores an instruction. Is the app broken?
A. Not automatically. Image quality, model behavior, incompatible edits,
   workflow settings, and reference clarity can all affect results.

Q. Can the app guarantee that adult-oriented prompts will work?
A. No. The app only prepares text; individual models and services determine
   which requests can be processed and what images they produce.

55. SIX PRINCIPLES TO REMEMBER
------------------------------------------------------------

FIRST: image1 is the final target image. Editing takes place there.

SECOND: image2 and image3 supply reference features, and their meaning comes
from the particular preset you select.

THIRD: the English preview is the editing instruction used by Qwen Image Edit.
The Korean descriptions in KO mode help a human choose presets, not the model.

FOURTH: one or two well-chosen, compatible instructions may be more effective
than a long collection of unrelated or conflicting edits.

FIFTH: when combining difficult face, outfit, pose, background, and detail
changes, consider breaking the work into separate editing passes.

SIXTH: a correct prompt still depends on the model, source-image clarity,
workflow compatibility, seed, and other runtime settings.

The bilingual interface does not alter those principles. Whether you choose
KO or EN, check the exact image1/image2/image3 references in the English
preview before launching an edit.
